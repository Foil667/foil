## Finding 2
On September 17, 2026, Elliptic launched Decode, an AI agent built on Elliptic's intelligence platform that answers natural-language questions about cryptoasset addresses and their exposure, returning the evidence behind every answer. Example questions include "Who does this address belong to, and what is it exposed to?", "Show the top stablecoin transfers to sanctioned entities in March 2026," and "Who are the top counterparties of this exchange by volume over the last six months?" Answers come back as ranked tables with a plain-language summary, each carrying the query that produced it and showing how the question was interpreted before it ran. Decode is the first Elliptic product built to the Elliptic Standard, a framework for governing agentic AI in on-chain risk that Elliptic published the previous week: Thinking Steps accompany every answer, the same question against the same data reaches the same conclusion, the agent does not close cases or choose where an investigation goes, and answers run on billions of labeled addresses Elliptic has built since 2013. Decode is currently live with government customers and will be available to the private sector later in the year. Elliptic CTO Jackson Hull: "The market is full of AI that asks to be trusted. That is not good enough when someone asks how you reached a decision."

## Finding 3
The ERC-8004 registry surface keeps growing but the standard is still unfinished. Per an April 2026 index audit cited on the page, roughly 15,000 of about 165,000 agents registered under ERC-8004 were dead or uncallable, so registration does not equal a working agent. The page states no publication date; it says its data is current as of July 2026. On status: ERC-8004 (EIP-8004: Trustless Agents, created 13 August 2025, co-authors from MetaMask, the Ethereum Foundation, Google, and Coinbase) remains a draft in 2026, with a version 2 specification in active development. Its reference implementation passes all 74 tests and runs on the Ethereum Sepolia testnet, while the EIP itself is not marked Final. Registry contracts are deployed across EVM chains including Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Gnosis, and Polygon, using CREATE2 for identical 0x8004-prefixed addresses everywhere. The Validation Registry is the least settled part of the standard; its official implementation carries a warning that the validation section remains under active update and discussion with the TEE community, with revisions expected in later spec updates. The page also notes the honest limits of the design: the specification admits Sybil attacks can inflate the scores of fake agents, and the reputation registry deliberately standardizes how feedback is posted and read rather than how it is scored.
