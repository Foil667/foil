## Finding 2
CryptoPawn, described in the release as a Delaware-licensed pawnbroker focused on digital assets, published an industry framework on September 21, 2026 (via Chainwire) setting four requirements before automated AI agents can take part in regulated crypto-backed transactions: identity, permission, collateral, and liability. On identity, the framework says an autonomous software agent is not automatically the legal customer in a financial transaction — the verified person or legal entity remains the contractual party and the agent acts only within granted authority — and cites the US federal E-SIGN Act on attributing an electronic agent's actions to the person being bound. On permission, it calls for defined limits on what an agent can pledge, how much it can borrow, agreement duration, and when human approval is required. On collateral, it says wallet access alone does not establish authority to pledge assets held in it. On liability, it says the contractual framework must determine responsibility when an agent exceeds its mandate or acts on outdated information, referencing NIST's AI-agent security work and the US Treasury's 2026 Financial Services AI Risk Management Framework. CryptoPawn's own model keeps the human or business customer as the verified contractual party, with a verified principal authorizing an agent to assist within defined permissions while remaining the borrower and owner of the pledged collateral. Relevant to the agent-identity beat as one of the first regulated-lending frameworks explicitly binding agent identity to human authority; caveat that this is company-issued wire copy, not independent reporting.
