## Finding 1
TRM Labs' September 2026 analysis of Coinbase's x402 agent-payments rail lands squarely on the identity problem: of $52.7 million settled across 198.9 million transactions on Base, Solana and Polygon since May 2025, TRM estimates only 0.6% to 7.5% of screened commerce (roughly $154,000 to $1.92 million) is plausibly AI-agent driven, a 2026 run-rate of $5,000-$11,000 per month. The structural diagnosis is the beat: ownership claims in on-chain agent registries, including ERC-8004-style claims, are voluntary, unverified and thinly used, so nothing in the protocol distinguishes a real agent from a cron job or load test. TRM's framing is not bearish on the rail; it argues the plumbing already works and what is missing is accurate registration plus counterparty reputation an agent can check on its own. Two other independent audits reached the same place: Visa and Artemis (published July 14, 2026) classified 89% of x402 dollar volume as wash, test or internal transfer, and an earlier Artemis analysis found 48% of transactions and 81% of dollar volume were gamed as of December 2025. USDC settled 99.6% of value ($52.47M of $52.68M). The page itself states no publication date; its comparison table dates the TRM report to September 2026.

## Finding 2
MemeToro, a planned AI-led fair-launch platform on BNB Chain, has open-sourced 1,373 lines of Solidity across 17 files covering its fair-launch escrow, executor interface, token-claim functions, tests and contributor rules, and says its presale has passed $139,000 in Stage 7 at $0.00430 per $MT token. The project's design has an AI agent research trends and propose launches via a documented launch manifest (proposed token structure, price, funding cap, data sources), with validators rejecting proposals that fail basic rules such as unsupported URLs or insider allocation above zero. The remaining milestones before mainnet include completing the token executor, BNB Chain testnet deployment, independent security review, and explicitly 'establishing ERC-8004 agent identity' - a live example of an agent launchpad wiring the ERC-8004 identity standard into its stack. The launchpad is still under development and the project is treating the AI agent as a roadmap feature until a live product ships.

## Finding 3
Elliptic has launched Decode, an AI agent built on its on-chain intelligence platform that answers natural-language questions about crypto addresses and their exposure in seconds, returning the evidence behind every answer: ranked data tables, the query that produced them, and 'Thinking Steps' showing how each conclusion was reached, so a reviewer, auditor or regulator can inspect the method. The launch follows Elliptic's publication last week of the Elliptic Standard, a framework for governing agentic AI in on-chain risk, and Decode is the first Elliptic solution built to it. CTO Jackson Hull said 'the market is full of AI that asks to be trusted. That is not good enough when someone asks how you reached a decision.' Decode is currently live with government customers and will be available to the private sector later in 2026. Relevance to the beat: it is a real deployed example of the identity-reputation thesis - an agent whose trust model is verifiable work rather than an unverifiable claim.
