## Finding 1
Launch day, September 22, produced readiness announcements rather than live trading. PrimeDelta Corp. announced a Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV) 'readiness program' following the SEC's September 17 Innovation Exemption order, mapping its identity, custody, liquidity-pool, settlement, and shareholder-rights stack to the framework. But the release says PrimeDelta 'has not yet provided notice to the SEC and is not currently operating a TSV,' and its next steps are to complete a legal assessment and prepare the notice. CEO Jason Lake said, 'Last Thursday the SEC drew the blueprint for how U.S. stocks can trade onchain. Our job now is execution.' No venue reported beginning pilot trading on day one; the exemption operates on a notice basis and the SEC does not approve individual venues.

## Finding 2
A key reason launch day stayed quiet: venues must notify issuers and wait before trading. The SEC order takes effect immediately and is not a formal rulemaking, but platforms must notify companies before listing tokenized versions of their stocks and are barred from offering the products if the issuer objects, an SEC official told Reuters. An SEC spokesperson told CNBC that venues should wait 30 days after a company receives notice before trading the token, and cannot list it if the company objects within that period. Tokens must carry the same rights as the underlying shares, including dividends and voting, synthetic derivative-style tokens are not permitted, and the exemption carries volume limits. The same piece notes Robinhood said this week it plans to let stock-token holders redeem tokens for underlying shares one-to-one and add voting rights, and Securitize shares rose as much as 24 percent on Thursday after the order, up 14 percent at last check.
- https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/investments/alternative-investments/us-sec-clears-tokenized-stock-trading-under-five-year-exemption/393575 (published 2026-09-21)

## Finding 3
Morningstar puts the scale and the incumbents' response in fresh numbers: the market for tokenized stocks has grown more than 500% in the past year, to $3 billion of stock tokenized as of Monday, September 21, about 0.5% of the Nasdaq's average daily volume last week, per rwa.xyz. The New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq are both developing token trading platforms, and the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation, custodian of more than $114 trillion in assets, plans to launch its tokenization service next month. The piece also reports a concrete skeptic's case from Duke lecturer Lee Reiners: the SEC exempted token venues from the best-execution requirement, which 'leaves open the possibility that customer trades will be executed at stale prices,' and it cites the Wall Street Journal reporting some tokens trading at 4 times the underlying share price. The page states no publication date, so the story is filed undated.
